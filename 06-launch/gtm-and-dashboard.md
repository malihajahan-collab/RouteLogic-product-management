# RouteLogic Velocity — GTM Strategy & Success Dashboard

> **Launch objective:** Protect at-risk enterprise accounts by restoring confidence in RouteLogic as the frontline system of action.

---

## Business Objective

Protect renewal revenue in at-risk enterprise accounts by restoring confidence in RouteLogic as the frontline system of action.

Velocity is not primarily an acquisition or awareness launch. The immediate business problem is existing customers questioning whether RouteLogic still delivers enough operational value to justify renewal.

---

## Launch Strategy

Use an **account-focused rollout**, beginning with the customers where frontline friction creates the greatest business risk.

### Primary User

**Fleet Coordinators and Dispatch Leads**  
The daily users whose behavior must change for Velocity to succeed.

### Economic Stakeholder

**VP Operations / Logistics Leaders**  
The decision-makers who need evidence that RouteLogic is improving operational value before renewal.

### Recommended Motion

1. **Pilot** with 3 enterprise accounts.
2. **Prove** measurable workflow improvement before making broader claims.
3. **Enable Sales and Customer Success** with before/after evidence.
4. **Use customer proof in renewal conversations.**
5. **Expand selectively** once the product signal supports the investment.

> **PM judgment:** The launch strategy follows the business risk. Concentrate effort where product friction is threatening account value rather than maximizing broad reach.

## Positioning

### Core Message

> **RouteLogic Velocity restores frontline speed without sacrificing the enterprise capabilities operations leaders depend on.**

### Proof Points

- Fewer steps in critical workflows
- Less reliance on calls, WhatsApp, spreadsheets, and screenshots
- Enterprise controls remain available in the background
- Product decisions are validated against speed, trust, and operational accuracy

## Launch Sequence

### Phase 1 — Pilot & Proof
**Weeks 1–4**

- Launch with 3 pilot accounts
- Capture adoption, friction, and customer feedback
- CS midpoint checkpoint in week 2
- Go / iterate / kill decision in week 4
- Build proof points before broader communication

### Phase 2 — Enterprise Launch
**Weeks 5–6**

- Roll out to eligible existing accounts
- Activate in-app and email communication
- Equip Sales and CS with account-specific messaging
- Use pilot evidence in customer conversations

### Phase 3 — Adoption & Renewal
**Week 7 onward**

- Monitor target-account adoption
- Review churn-related escalations
- Track renewal outcomes as accounts enter their renewal windows
- Feed launch learning back into the roadmap

## Success Dashboard

The launch should be measured at **business, product, and operational levels** rather than by launch activity alone.

| KPI | Level | Baseline | Target | Measurement Window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **At-risk account retention** | Business / North Star | Current retention of flagged accounts | Improve vs. comparable pre-launch cohort | Renewal cycle |
| **Velocity adoption in target accounts** | Product | 0% before launch | Pre-commit target after pilot baseline | First 30 days |
| **Churn-related support / escalation volume** | Operational | Pre-launch account baseline | Reduce vs. baseline | 30–60 days |

> **Metric discipline:** Numeric launch targets should be committed using actual account and pilot baselines. They are not invented for this case study.

## How My PM Input Strengthened the GTM Plan

A generic XL launch could easily become a broad marketing campaign.

I reframed the plan around the actual business problem: **retention risk inside the existing enterprise base.**

| Generic Launch Logic | PM Reframe |
|---|---|
| Maximize reach | Reach the accounts most at risk |
| Broad paid media | Prioritize Sales, CS, in-app, and account-based communication |
| Lead with new features | Lead with restored speed and trust |
| Measure launch activity | Measure retention, adoption, and escalation signals |
| Treat launch day as the finish line | Manage pilot → launch → post-launch adoption as one learning cycle |

---

## Post-Launch Decision Framework

- **Double down:** adoption is sustained and renewal signals improve
- **Iterate:** interest exists, but activation or usage stalls
- **Pivot:** target accounts respond to a different value proposition than expected
- **Deprioritize:** weak adoption and no meaningful business signal after learning is captured

---

## GTM Takeaway

> **Velocity is not a campaign for a new feature. It is a coordinated retention strategy built around restoring customer confidence in the core product.**

The launch succeeds when at-risk customers see measurable evidence that RouteLogic has become easier to operate without becoming less capable.
